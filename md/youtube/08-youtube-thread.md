YouTube test features and experiments
What are YouTube experiments?
YouTube product teams are constantly testing out new tools and features to help you find, watch, share and create content more easily. These test features (sometimes also called “betas” or “experiments”) are usually available for a short period of time and only for a small group of people.
If you ever notice that YouTube looks a bit different for you than it does for your friend or family member, you’re likely seeing one of these experiments. Be sure to give YouTube feedback and report any potential technical issues – our teams use this information and experiment results to help us decide if, when, and how to release these features more broadly.
Learn more about some existing feature experiments
To help you keep up with these test features, our TeamYouTube Community Managers will pilot keeping this forum thread updated with cool and interesting ones that we think you’ll want to know about! While this post won’t include every experiment, it’s a good place to start if you’re curious about what we’re testing out, or whether or not you’re currently seeing an experiment. To learn about previous experiments, check out this post .

[April 27, 2026] Testing a new search experience on YouTube: We’re testing a conversational search experience to complement how you already search on YouTube. Our goal is to help you dive deeper into the topics you’re curious about in a more interactive way.
If you’re in the experiment, you can try it out by selecting "Ask YouTube" in the search bar. For example, you can ask for help planning a 3-day road trip from San Francisco to Santa Barbara, and you'll get a structured, step-by-step itinerary instead of a list of videos. The response will bring together a new mix of long-form videos, Shorts, and informative text featuring local tips and must-see stops. You can ask follow-up questions like, "where can I find good coffee?" to explore local spots along your route. We'll surface videos and relevant video segments, accompanied by their titles and channel details, to make it easy to discover new creators and jump into the most helpful content from your search. This experiment is currently available for YouTube Premium members 18+ in the US who opt-in via youtube.com/new . We’re working on expanding the experiment to non-Premium users in the future.

[April 30, 2026] Testing a new comment search tool in Studio: We’re experimenting with a new tool in YouTube Studio that uses AI to help creators more easily find and moderate comments. Instead of only searching for specific keywords, this tool helps you find, manage, and respond to comments based on their broader topic or meaning. If you are in the experiment group, you will see a new "Search" filter (the old search field is now labeled as "Keywords"). Here’s what you can do:
Search by topic: Use your own words to find specific topics (up to 100 characters). For example, you can search for “questions about my gear” or “people asking for a part 2 video.”
Use suggested topics: Select suggested topics to quickly filter for themes like "Excitement and enthusiasm" or "Negative feedback."
Find similar comments: Click the three-dot menu on a specific comment to easily "Find similar comments."
This tool does not automatically remove or hide comments; it simply helps you find them. You are still in control of deciding what moderation actions to take on your comments. This is currently rolling out to a small group of creators on Studio desktop. If you have access, try it out and let us know your thoughts !

[July 16, 2026] Testing a streamlined, insights-first experience in YouTube Studio: We’re kicking off an experiment on desktop designed to help you quickly spot trends and get deeper insights on how your content is performing. Here’s a look at what’s changing in YouTube Studio for those in the experiment group:
What to expect during this test:
Insights-first experience in Studio: We’re renaming the "Analytics" tab in YouTube Studio to Insights.
Fresh design: We’ve made it easier to interpret your performance by grouping relevant metrics and charts into an updated user interface.
Streamlined navigation: To switch between sub pages, you may use the new back button to navigate to the Insights overview page. Click on any chart or metric to see more in Advanced Mode.
Simplified date range: To simplify your main view, we’ve trimmed the date range selector on the overview page. Don't worry—your full realtime data is still fully available under the range selector, and all custom date ranges are accessible by clicking on any card to enter Advanced Mode.
AI-powered insight cards: If you’re eligible for Ask Studio, select creators will be able to test four new AI cards—Channel summary, Content patterns, Audience loyalty, and Video summary.
Research: We’re experimenting with evolving the Trends tab in Studio into a destination called Research — with tools to help inspire your next video.
Note: Evolving to this content-first model means the "saved keywords" feature on desktop is being retired. If you have keywords saved, you can access them in Studio mobile, or download them as a CSV file over the next three months via the link in your Research notification by clicking “download keywords”.
This experiment is currently rolling out to a small group of creators, and we’ll keep you updated on any expansion plans!

[August 24, 2026] Try creating multiple custom feeds on your YouTube mobile app or desktop: Building on our May launch of custom feeds , we are expanding this feature to allow you to build and save multiple custom feeds, pinned to the top of your Home feed. If you're part of this experiment, here’s how you can try it out:
Get started: Swipe left or click the "custom feed" tab at the top of your Home screen in the mobile app or desktop to create your feed.
Pick a vibe: Type a prompt describing what you want to see to instantly generate your personalized feed, such as “help me unwind after work with guided meditations under 10 minutes.”
Switch between feeds: Your new custom feeds stay saved at the top of your Home page so you can easily switch between them. You can keep up to eight active feeds at a time, and inactive prompts will naturally expire after a few weeks.
Edit prompts: You can edit your prompt directly in the text box at the top of your feed to adjust your dedicated discovery space whenever you want.
Note: If you are using the mobile app, you will notice that custom feeds are replacing standard topic chips at the top of your Home feed. Custom feeds can be used in a similar way as chips to focus recommendations on one or multiple topics. If you prefer classic topic chips, they are still available when browsing YouTube on desktop.
This experiment is currently rolling out to signed-in viewers on mobile (Android and iOS) and desktop in the U.S. with English language settings and watch history turned on. If you're in the test, try it out and share your feedback by clicking the “Feedback” link under the prompt.

[September 3, 2026] Expanding the Watch With live stream feature to more streamers: We’re testing an expansion of the Watch With feature, making it easy for eligible mobile creators to go live and share live reactions alongside other creators’ live streams across YouTube.
How it works for reacting creators:
If you're in the experiment group, you will see a "React live" button in the Share panel when viewing eligible streams on your mobile device. Tapping this button lets you broadcast your own reaction alongside the original content. Your phone camera will automatically lock in a horizontal orientation, and you can’t turn your camera off for the reaction. You will be able to earn revenue as normal on your reactions. Viewers can tune in from any device, and will see both your camera view and the source video combined as a vertical stream.
How it works for source creators:
If you’re the original creator—the creator streaming the content others are reacting to—you will continue to earn revenue from your streams as normal. Revenue from reactions is not shared, however, the concurrent viewership metrics, public views, and watch time from all the reactions will be attributed back to your content. By default, eligible streams are opted in to allow other creators to react. If you prefer not to have others react to your content, you can easily opt out: make sure to update your app and before you go live, change the "React live" setting in your mobile metadata editor from "Allow eligible channels" to "No one".
This feature is currently rolling out as an experiment. If you have access , give it a try on your next stream and let us know how it goes at http://yt.be/help/feedback – we’d love to hear your feedback!
